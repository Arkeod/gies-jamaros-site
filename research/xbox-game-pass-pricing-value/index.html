Xbox Game Pass Pricing in 2026: What Each Tier Costs and Whether It Is Worth It

Business ModelsGame PassJune 27, 2026

Microsoft rolled Xbox Game Pass Ultimate back to $22.99 per month in April 2026, reversing the $29.99 price set months earlier after subscriber backlash. The reversal is the most important pricing signal the subscription has sent since launch, and it reframes the value question for anyone deciding between a subscription and buying games outright.

Xbox Game Pass tiers and prices in 2026

Game Pass now ships in four tiers. The headline numbers, as of June 2026:

TierMonthly priceCore proposition
Essential$9.99Smaller library, cloud gaming, online multiplayer
PC Game Pass$13.99300+ PC games, day-one first-party, EA Play
Premium$14.99200+ games, console and PC, cloud gaming
Ultimate$22.99Full library, day-one first-party, cloud, EA Play

Ultimate sits at $22.99 after the rollback from $29.99. Essential ($9.99) and Premium ($14.99) held their prices through the change, while PC Game Pass moved to $13.99.

What the rollback signals

A price reversal this fast is a churn signal, not a confidence signal. Microsoft pushed Ultimate to $29.99, watched the response, and retreated. For investors the read is that the subscription has less pricing power at the top tier than the 2025 increase assumed. The ceiling on Ultimate is closer to $23 than $30 in the current market.

The second signal is content. New Call of Duty titles no longer launch day-one on the service. Removing the single biggest annual draw from the top tier while cutting its price tells you Microsoft is managing the unit economics of day-one first-party content, not just chasing subscriber count.

Subscription versus buying: the value math

At $22.99 per month, Ultimate costs about $276 per year. A buyer who finishes three to four full-price titles a year now pays a comparable amount once AAA standard pricing reaches $79.99 (see GTA 6 pricing). The subscription wins for players with high title throughput and for day-one first-party releases. It loses for selective buyers who play a small number of games deeply and keep them. The CoD change weakens the subscription case for the largest annual-franchise audience specifically.

The strategic question for the industry is whether $79.99 standalone pricing makes subscriptions look cheaper by comparison, or whether it trains buyers to wait for the game to hit the service. Microsoft is betting on the former. The price rollback suggests the market has not fully agreed.
