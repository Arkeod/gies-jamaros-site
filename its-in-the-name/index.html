How to write a pitch the tool understands

Every comparable below comes from matching your words against the tags and titles of 30,000 Steam games. Words that are Steam tags carry weight; story, scenario and marketing prose carry none. Measured on 80 test pitches, a mechanics-first description found the right neighbors about twice as often as the same game's store blurb.

Include, in this order of importance:

  • Genre, in Steam's words. One to three tags: roguelike deckbuilder, city builder, tactical FPS, farming sim, point & click.
  • What the player does. Three verbs that are also tags: chop, craft, automate, trade, survive, explore, build, cook.
  • How it is seen. First-person, third-person, top-down, side-scrolling, 2D, pixel art, hand-drawn, anime, VR.
  • Mode and session. Single-player, co-op, PvP, local multiplayer; short runs, open world, campaign.
  • Setting and mood. Post-apocalyptic, WWII, space, pirates; cozy, comedic, grim, horror.
  • Two games it sits between. Written as titles: "like Stardew Valley meets FTL". Two-word titles pin reliably; the named game's tag profile joins the match.

Leave out: plot and scenario ("your parents are on their way home"), your own world and company names, adjectives like unique or immersive. Length without tags is noise: 200 characters of tags beat 500 of prose.
